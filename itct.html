IT Concepts and Tools

Semester 2 Class 2026

What the class was about

ITCT5.120 IT Concepts and Tools gave me a familiarity with the range of IT tools and concepts used in organisations, with a focus on professional communication. It covered the components of IT and the five critical IT functions, information systems software (office support and management information systems), IT roles and processes, organisational structure and culture, and the positive and negative impacts of IT on business. The course had three assignments. Assignment 1 included a personal knowledge management (PKM) system, a group online office manual for a start-up called The Harvesting Collective, an individual business report, and an online portfolio of my BCS courses. Assignment 2 was a set of Microsoft Excel labs. Assignment 3 was a set of presentations: a short presentation on a non-IT topic, an Excel presentation, and a longer formal presentation on an IT topic.

What I learnt

In this course I learned how IT supports an organisation as a whole, from the hardware, software, networks, and cloud services a business relies on to the roles and processes that keep them running. I learned to use a range of tools for different jobs, including Word for formal business reports, Copilot for research (with the need to disclose and check AI output), Ruminate for weighted decision matrices when comparing options like CRM systems, Visio and Miro for flowcharts, swim-lane process maps, and mind maps, and a PKM tool to organise my notes and time. I learned to analyse an organisation's culture and its impact, using SWOT analysis and the idea that new technology means change rather than automatic progress. In Excel, I learned to go beyond basic spreadsheets and use more powerful features, and my presentation on Goal Seek and Solver taught me how to work backwards from a target to find the inputs needed to reach it. Collaborating on a shared online office manual taught me to agree on a tool, divide the work, and write documentation for the future employees who will use it. Preparing three presentations taught me how to research a topic, structure it for a set time limit (3 minutes for my passion topic and Excel talk, 7 minutes for my tech talk), and explain it clearly to an audience, whether it was the science behind luck or how Near Field Communication (NFC) works. Throughout, I learned to write professionally, caption and organise figures consistently, reference in APA, and acknowledge AI use.

What I achieved

In Assignment 1, I set up a personal knowledge management and time-management system to organise my coursework and my presentation notes, and wrote a report describing the tool, its strengths and weaknesses, and my reflection on how well it worked. As part of a group, I helped build an online office manual for The Harvesting Collective using a collaborative workspace tool, covering its IT tools and systems, critical IT functions, culture, mission and values, and ethical and professional conduct. I also wrote a personal reflection on my contribution and my group's. For my individual business report, I used Word, Copilot, Visio, Ruminate, and Miro to cover the components of IT across organisations, the five critical IT functions and their challenges, office support systems, CRM decision matrices, IT roles and an IT operations organisation chart, a software request flowchart, a swim-lane process map of an online purchase, a SWOT analysis of an organisation's culture, and a mind map of IT's positive and negative impacts. I built this online portfolio and submitted a formal memo to my tutor, along with my reflection. In Assignment 2, I completed three Microsoft Excel lab activities, which gave me a stronger foundation as an Excel user. In Assignment 3, I delivered three presentations. My 3-minute passion topic explained the science behind luck. My 3-minute Excel presentation demonstrated Goal Seek and Solver. My 7-minute technology presentation covered Near Field Communication (NFC).

Reflections

This course showed me that using IT well is about choosing the right tool for the job and then communicating clearly about it. Trying so many tools in a short time, from decision matrices to swim-lane maps, helped me see how each one organises information differently, and it taught me to double-check AI-generated information instead of trusting it blindly. Working in a group on the office manual showed me how much shared documentation depends on clear roles, consistent formatting, and regular check-ins, and I'd plan our structure and workload earlier next time. The presentations were the most challenging part, because I had to cut a topic like NFC or the science behind luck down to its key ideas and fit them within the time limit, so I'd like to keep practising my pacing and delivery. Overall, I now feel more confident using professional tools, writing business reports, and presenting technical topics to an audience, which will help me in team and client-facing IT roles.
